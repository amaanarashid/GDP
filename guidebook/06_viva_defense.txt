==============================================================
06 — VIVA PREP: DEFENDING THE ML MODELS
==============================================================

THE ONE-SENTENCE PITCH
----------------------
"Trained only on healthy data, our detector caught a real
oil-leak failure it had never seen 51 hours before the repair,
with a 1.3% false-alarm rate — evaluated on a strict temporal
hold-out of a real metro compressor."


THE THREE MODELS AND WHY
------------------------

1) RandomForest — Dataset Lab classifier (/train, /predict)
   Why: our datasets are small tabular data (AI4I = 10k rows, 5
   sensors). On tabular data this size, tree ensembles match or
   beat deep learning — Grinsztajn et al. 2022, "Why do
   tree-based models still outperform deep learning on tabular
   data".
   - Handles class imbalance via class weighting (failures are
     under 4% of rows)
   - predict_proba gives the risk % shown in the UI
   - Feature importance = explainable to a maintenance engineer
   - No scaling/tuning fragility — users drop in ARBITRARY
     datasets
   - An LSTM would overfit ~300 failure examples and give
     nothing interpretable

2) IsolationForest — failure detector (THE HEADLINE RESULT)
   Why: the real constraint is labels. A new machine has no
   failure history, and MetroPT-3 has only TWO failure events
   before our test window — too few for supervised learning.
   So we learn "normal" instead.
   - Zero labels, trains in seconds
   - Assumes no particular failure signature — exactly why it
     caught an OIL LEAK, a type absent from training
   - We tested the supervised alternative on the same window:
     AUC ~0.5 (random). The unsupervised detector: 0.91.
     THE DATA DECIDED, NOT US. (Strongest viva answer — we
     tried the alternative and say so.)
   - Features: 2-hour rolling duty cycles of 8 signals. An air
     leak makes the compressor run more often — that shows in
     multi-hour aggregates, not 10-minute windows (we tested
     10-min windows first: no signal).

3) HistGradientBoosting quantile regression + CQR — RUL
   Why: two training failures means point RUL can't be trusted,
   so we output DISTRIBUTIONS, not points.
   - P10/P50/P90 models + Conformalized Quantile Regression
     (Romano et al. 2019), calibrated on a temporal hold-out
   - 78% coverage vs 80% target on unseen failures — honest
     uncertainty instead of false precision
   - UI shows "8–15 days", never "12.3 days"


THE UNIFYING THEME (if pushed on "why not deep learning")
---------------------------------------------------------
"We matched model complexity to data volume. Deep models need
thousands of failure examples; industrial PdM datasets have
handfuls. Our contribution isn't a fancy architecture — it's a
rigorous evaluation protocol: temporal splits, no leakage,
unseen failure types, false-alarm rates reported."


LIKELY FOLLOW-UPS AND ANSWERS
-----------------------------
Q: Why not XGBoost?
A: HistGradientBoosting is the same algorithm family,
   sklearn-native, one fewer dependency.

Q: Why not an autoencoder for anomaly detection?
A: Tried-and-true baseline first. With 8 features and one
   machine, an autoencoder adds parameters without adding
   signal. Valid future work.

Q: How did you pick hyperparameters?
A: Deliberately near-default to avoid overfitting the tiny
   test set. Tuning on 2 test failures would be leakage by
   another name.

Q: Random split vs chronological split?
A: Random splits on time-series leak the future and inflate
   metrics. Switching AI4I to chronological dropped F1 from
   0.72 to 0.57 — the lower number is the defensible one.
   AUC stays ~0.97.

Q: Isn't 4.4 days MAE for RUL bad?
A: On the full window a naive "always predict max" scores
   better MAE because machines are mostly healthy. MAE alone is
   the wrong lens: near failure (last 72h) the model beats
   naive baselines, and the calibrated interval is the useful
   output. We say this openly. The detector is the operational
   early-warning; RUL is the planning aid.

Q: Only 2 test failures — enough to claim anything?
A: It's ALL the real failures in the test period, one of a type
   never seen in training — the hardest generalization test.
   AUC is over ~54k test minutes, plus the false-alarm rate.


WHAT'S REAL vs WHAT'S ESTIMATED (volunteer this first)
------------------------------------------------------
REAL, VALIDATED ML:
  - MetroPT-3 detector: AUC 0.91, unseen real failures
  - AI4I classifier: chronological split, honest metrics
  - MetroPT-3 machine-level RUL: defensible BECAUSE we report
    weak MAE and a calibrated interval

ENGINEERING ESTIMATES (stated openly in the app):
  1. MetroPT per-COMPONENT RUL = the machine-level prediction
     applied to each component ("component granularity needs
     per-component failure data, which MetroPT-3 doesn't have").
  2. The risk% -> days bands in /predict are an indicative
     mapping, not learned.
  3. Simulated components' "days left" = degradation-trend
     extrapolation (trendRul.js): health margin / measured
     decline rate. We REMOVED a neural net that trained on
     labels from our own formula — circular. Say proudly:
     "we deliberately do NOT use ML where it can't be
     validated."

Why volunteering wins: examiners probe until they find the soft
spot. Name it first, with the correct engineering term, and the
weakness becomes evidence of rigor.


DATASET EXPORT (Simulate -> Export CSV)
---------------------------------------
Labels derived from operational events: failure = critical
alert within the next N minutes (5/10/30 selectable).
  - A real plant has no ground-truth flags — only sensors plus
    an event log. This is the DEPLOYABLE pipeline.
  - The horizon N is a business decision: the warning time the
    maintenance team needs to act.
  - Label noise mirrors real work-order data.
  - Sanity check if asked: we can compare derived labels to the
    simulator's injection ground truth — a luxury real plants
    don't have, which is why the pipeline doesn't depend on it.
Caveat: models trained on simulated data validate the WORKFLOW;
model validation remains MetroPT-3 and AI4I.


KEY NUMBERS (memorize)
----------------------
Detector: AUC 0.906 · oil leak flagged +70 min = ~51 h before
  repair (type not in training) · air leak +45 min · false
  alarms 1.3% · trained on healthy Feb–May 2020 only, no labels
RUL: P50 MAE 4.37 d (cap 14 d) · 80% interval coverage 78% ·
  train F1+F2 (air leaks), test F3 (oil leak) + F4
AI4I: F1 0.571 · ROC-AUC 0.973 · chronological by UDI, last 20%
  (random-split F1 was 0.72 — inflated by leakage)
Datasets: MetroPT-3 = real metro compressor, Porto 2020, ~10s
  cadence, 4 documented failures · AI4I = 10k rows, 3.4% failure


THE PROJECT STORY ARC (report intro)
------------------------------------
Started with simulation to prove the pipeline works -> realized
simulated data can't teach failure prediction -> added real-data
upload with supervised learning -> kept anomaly detection for
machines without failure history -> validated on real datasets
with leakage-safe evaluation. The lecturer's "use real data"
feedback becomes a chapter, not a criticism.
