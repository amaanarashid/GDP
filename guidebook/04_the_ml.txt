==============================================================
04 — THE MACHINE LEARNING, HONESTLY EXPLAINED
==============================================================

There are three models plus one deliberate non-model. Each
answers a different question.

"IS SOMETHING WRONG RIGHT NOW?"  — anomaly detector
  Studies weeks of HEALTHY behaviour, then scores how weird the
  current readings look (0–100). Needs zero failure examples —
  which matters, because new machines have no failure history.
  THE PROJECT'S HEADLINE RESULT: trained only on healthy data
  from a real metro air compressor (MetroPT-3), it caught a
  real oil leak — a failure type it had NEVER seen — about 70
  minutes after it started, ~51 hours before the repair was
  finished. AUC 0.906, false alarms 1.3% of healthy minutes.
  Same technique runs on simulated machines from their own DB
  history ("Train on history" on the machine page).

"WILL IT FAIL SOON?"  — failure classifier
  For datasets WITH a failure column (AI4I, or your exported
  CSVs). A RandomForest — hundreds of decision trees voting —
  learns which sensor patterns precede failure and outputs a
  risk %. Also shows which sensors matter (feature importance).
  Evaluated with a CHRONOLOGICAL split (train on the past, test
  on the future) — random splits leak the future and inflate
  scores; when we fixed this, F1 dropped from 0.72 to 0.57 and
  the lower number is the honest one (AUC ~0.97).

"HOW LONG UNTIL IT FAILS?"  — RUL with uncertainty
  Predicts days-until-failure as a RANGE ("8–15 days"), never a
  single number. Three quantile models (P10/P50/P90) plus
  conformal calibration; the truth lands inside the 80% band
  78% of the time on unseen failures. With only two training
  failures, pretending to know "12.3 days" would be lying —
  the calibrated range is the honest version.

"DAYS LEFT" ON SIMULATED PARTS  — deliberately NOT ML
  Trend extrapolation: health margin (current health minus the
  20% service threshold) divided by how fast health is falling
  right now. Wear speeds up with machine age and jumps when
  sensors go warning/critical. We removed a neural net that
  used to do this — it trained on labels made from our own
  formula (circular reasoning dressed as ML). An estimate you
  can explain in one sentence beats a black box you can't
  defend.

THE ONE RULE
  Numbers from real data are the proof; numbers from simulated
  machines demonstrate the workflow. Never mix the two claims.

WHERE "PREDICTIVE" LIVES (the when)
  - Countdown: RUL days / days-left per component
  - Lead time: alarm date vs repair date on real data (51 h)
  - Risk %: probability of failure within a defined horizon —
    the time is baked into the question, not the axis
